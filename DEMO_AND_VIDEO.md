# Turnout: Demo Data, Judge Walkthrough, and Video Script

This file was rewritten after the system was built, so that every line matches what it actually
does. Where the original plan and the running system disagreed, the system won and the script
changed. Two places where that happened are called out below, because they are worth knowing.

---

## 1. Synthetic dataset

Generated by `src/turnout/data/generate.py`, seeded, committed as
`data/scenarios/demo_week.json` so judges see the same week we recorded.

### 1.1 Departments

| Department | Members | Districts | Role in the story |
|---|---|---|---|
| Millbrook Volunteer Fire Company | 14 | north, south | The department the story follows. Chief Dana Ortiz. Thin daytime coverage. |
| Riverton Fire and Rescue | 22 | central, east | Better staffed on weekdays. Their chief has pre-approved mutual aid where the delay is under 10 minutes and the ledger stays within 8 hours. |
| Cedar Hollow Volunteer Fire Department | 11 | west | The weakest of the three, and at risk in the same window, which is why it declines. |

### 1.2 Members and history

Each member has roles, certifications, quiet hours, and a response history by window type built
from one of four archetypes: retiree, commuter, shift worker, mixed. Twelve months of calls are
generated per department from an hour-of-day and day-of-week profile shaped like the national
pattern, with each call's responders drawn from those same probabilities, so the history the risk
engine learns from is coherent with the availability it predicts.

Phone numbers are all `+1555…`, which is the reserved fictional range.

### 1.3 The demo week

The clock starts **Wednesday 9 September 2026 at 06:30**. An ice storm warning covers Thursday
06:00 to 14:00, injected by the scenario file so the same story plays every time. The live weather
tool calls the public National Weather Service API; the landing page says which is which.

## 2. What actually happens when you press the steps

This is a transcript of a real run, not a plan.

| Step | What the system does |
|---|---|
| **Roll call** (Wed 06:30) | One text to each of the 14 members about Thursday. Replies come back over the next hour: `Y`, `N`, `till 10`, `after 3`, `morning only`, and one member who writes "depends on the kids, probably not". Two members never reply. |
| **Coverage pass** (Wed 07:30) | Watch scores the week. Thursday 10:00 to 14:00 north is **critical**: under one call expected, short a driver and a firefighter, 100 percent chance nobody qualified responds, ice storm warning. Closer asks the two members most likely to say yes for that window. One of them is in quiet hours until 08:00, so the hook holds that message and it goes out later, tagged. |
| **Ask the neighbours** (Wed 08:45) | One of the asks succeeded: a driver said yes. The window is still short a firefighter, so Neighbor sends a structured request to both peers. Riverton offers, 9 minute delay. Cedar Hollow declines: "own west district at high risk in that window". Chief Gate batches Thursday's two open windows into **one** text to the chief. |
| **The chief decides** (Wed 09:15) | The chief replies `1`. Turnout confirms with Riverton, whose agent auto-approves inside its own chief's rule without waking anyone, and both ledgers move. Millbrook now owes Riverton 7 hours. |
| **After the call** (Thu 13:00) | A collision on Thursday. The officer leaves a voice debrief. Scribe drafts the NERIS report and flags the fields it was unsure about rather than guessing them. |

### Two places the script changed to match the system

**"1 to 2 calls expected" became "under 1 call expected".** A four hour window in a department that
runs about 1.6 calls a day genuinely expects less than one call. The window is still critical,
because the risk engine multiplies by a scale constant reflecting that a chief treats an uncovered
window in an ice storm as critical regardless. Saying "1 to 2 calls" in the video when the screen
says "under 1" would have been a small lie in the one place the project claims to be honest.

**"There is no driver" became "Closer found a driver, and they are still short a firefighter".**
Closer actually succeeded in recruiting a driver from the targeted asks. That is a better story: it
shows the agent doing its own job first and only escalating what it genuinely cannot solve.

## 3. Judge walkthrough, about four minutes

1. Open the live URL. Read the hero, then press **Open the live demo**.
2. Press the five steps in order, or press **Play the rest of the week** and watch it run itself.
   Each step takes 10 to 40 seconds, because real agents are running. A step that is not next is
   disabled: asking the neighbours before the coverage pass would send a request about a gap
   nothing has found yet.
3. **Station board**: each gap has a plain-language explanation. Expand *Show the numbers behind
   this* for expected calls, the weather multiplier, the probability nobody qualified responds, the
   time-critical weighting, how many days of history it used, and the formula with the numbers
   substituted in.
4. **Phones**: both sides of every text. The chief's thread shows one decision message for two
   windows. The reply buttons go through the same inbound path a real message would.
5. **Network**: the three departments, the mutual aid balances, and every A2A exchange in order.
6. **Agent trace**: every step, including the decision to hold a message for quiet hours and the
   decision to batch two windows into one interrupt.
7. **Incident report**: the NERIS draft with its uncertain fields flagged.
8. **What the agent asks of you** (`/crew.html`): the other side of the chief's board. For every
   member, how many times the agent has asked this week against the cap, the hours it will not text
   them, every message it sent including the ones it held, and the response history behind the two
   people it chose. An agent that asks unpaid people for their time should be answerable to them.
9. **Set up your department** (`/start.html`): paste any roster and see what was understood before
   anything is saved.
10. Press **Reset** to run it again.
